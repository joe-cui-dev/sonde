Every Saturday the night market took over the car park behind the old cinema, and every Saturday Dev's aunt set up her stall in the same spot, third row from the entrance, between the man who sold phone cases and the couple who fried dumplings in a pan the size of a cartwheel.

Dev had helped her since he was twelve. At first it was carrying crates; later it was taking money; by sixteen he could make the stall's one dish, a peppery lentil soup, almost as well as she could, though she never said so.

That summer the council announced the car park would be sold. There were meetings, a petition, a story in the local paper with a photo of the dumpling pan. None of it made any difference. The last market was set for the final Saturday in August.

On the last night, the market was busier than Dev had ever seen it. People came who had not been in years. His aunt sold out of soup by nine o'clock and spent the rest of the evening talking to customers across the empty pot. At midnight the generators went off one by one, and the stalls were packed into vans, and the car park was just a car park again.

Dev drove his aunt home. Neither of them said much.
